Case Study 01

SOAR — STLT Outbreak Analytics & Response

SOAR enables real-time analytics for the monitoring, modeling, and forecasting of infectious diseases — COVID-19, influenza, RSV, and West Nile virus — so state, tribal, local, and territorial public health officials, healthcare providers, and policy makers can make data-driven detection, mitigation, and response decisions. It runs at 94.8% prediction accuracy across 1.2M+ data points processed in real time. The live platform is at soar-forecasting.internationalrespondersystems.net.

Forecast, evaluate, and act — in one platform

Program-led delivery 33 models · 4 pathogens

Product and delivery lead · forecasting, MQAT, genomics, alerting

Most forecasting tools stop at a chart. The hard problems for a public health agency come after that: which of the 33 available models should I trust, how do I combine them, how do I know when a forecast crosses a line that demands action, and how do I connect what the lab is sequencing to what the model is predicting. SOAR answers all four.

Forecasting & model selection

  • Ensemble forecasting across COVID-19, RSV, influenza, and West Nile, with probabilistic quantile selection (P10–P98) and historical-forecast overlay
  • Build-your-own ensemble — combine up to five component models with user-assigned weights summing to 100%, then evaluate the result against the field
  • Automatic WIS evaluation in the data pipeline, so every model is continuously scored rather than trusted on reputation
  • Peak-week prediction for influenza, expressed as a dated probability

Quality, genomics & response

  • Model Quality Assessment Tool (MQAT) — ranks all 33 models against a multi-criteria decision framework with user-weighted criteria, saved filter sets, and transparent model cards
  • UShER phylogenetic analysis — upload VCF or FASTA sequences and place them on a phylogenetic tree via Taxonium
  • Lab-result reconciliation — compares state and county lab detections against sequencing outcomes per sample, scored by match rate
  • Threshold alerting over email and SMS when forecasted hospitalizations exceed a configured level, with a four-step authoring flow
Integrated operationally, not just visually — I delivered the forecasting API service with credential handling, a webhook receiver that auto-creates incidents from outbreak alerts, and generic event-type routing so new alert classes onboard without code changes. That closes the loop from forecast → alert → operational incident response.

Capability

  • Trend analytics — 2/4/8-week smoothing, confidence bands, a two-week linear forecast toggle, and a date scrubber

Burden & Demand Model — forecast to supply

Designed & built 160 hrs est → 30 hrs actual

RESP-NET aligned methodology · feeds the inventory and procurement layer

A forecast that stops at a case count doesn't help anyone order supplies. This model takes hospitalization input — entered manually or read directly from a SOAR forecast JSON — and carries it all the way through to how many vaccine doses and how many tests a jurisdiction should plan for, at low, mid, and high scenarios.

The chain it models

  • Seasonal hospitalization total from off-season, moderate, and peak weekly averages × peak duration
  • Age allocation across 0–17, 18–49, 50–64, and 65+ using population share and relative severity weights
  • Infection-to-hospitalization multipliers per age band, inverting hospitalizations back to true infections
  • Burden conversion — symptomatic rate of infections, outpatient rate of symptomatic
  • Demand scenarios — vaccine uptake and tests-per-symptomatic at low/mid/high, producing the procurement numbers

Validated, not asserted

  • RESP-NET validation panel built into the tool: the model's age distribution is checked against published RESP-NET benchmarks per age band, showing the delta in percentage points
  • In the run shown, all four age bands land within 3pp of benchmark — the model isn't just internally consistent, it reproduces the surveillance system it claims alignment with
  • Direct SOAR forecast JSON ingestion, so the epidemic forecast drives the supply number without a human retyping it
  • Pairs with the Incident Bill of Materials in the inventory module, which maps demand to incident type
Outcome — 160 conventionally-estimated hours delivered in 30. This is the piece that makes "forecast-driven supply" a real claim rather than a slogan — and it is the work accepted for presentation at a 2026 national public health laboratory conference.

Platform Scorecard — weighted competitive analysis

Designed & built 12 platforms · 16 measures · 7 stages

Competitive analysis tool · anonymized as "Acme" for publication

Competitive analysis usually arrives as a static slide that encodes one person's weighting and can't be argued with. This tool makes the weighting the point: score twelve life-sciences platforms across sixteen measures spanning seven lifecycle stages, then swap the lens — addressable market, strategic gaps, clinical-stage focus, or equal weights — and watch the ranking reorder.

How it works

  • Four weighting lenses, each a different strategic question, applied to the same underlying scores
  • A published scoring rubric — five performance levels from Very High to Very Low, with explicit grading criteria written out for every measure, so a score is defensible rather than asserted
  • Buyer personas modelled alongside the vendors, so the analysis can ask "who is this for" and not just "who is biggest"
  • Value factors from Very High (1.0) to Very Low (0.05), with every bar decomposable into its contributing measures

Where it goes deeper than a chart

  • Radar by lifecycle stage — shows shape, not just size: two platforms on similar totals can have completely different coverage profiles
  • Measure-detail comparison — every cell carries the score, its weighted contribution, and a written justification, including the honest ones ("not a differentiator and not in Acme's core scope")
  • It survives disagreement — when someone rejects the weighting you change the lens in front of them instead of rebuilding the deck
  • Under the addressable-market lens the lead platform reaches 75.0% against 60.0% and 59.4% for the nearest two, and the radar shows exactly which stages produce that lead

Portfolio Impact Dashboard

Designed & built Live market data · demo book

Real-time desktop analytics · personal project

A live equities dashboard built around one idea: position-weighted impact, not headline percentages. A large move on a small position matters less than a small move on a large one, so the treemap sizes every holding by position size × day change — what actually moved the portfolio.

Capability

  • Impact treemap sized by position and coloured by direction, so the session reads at a glance
  • Multiple named portfolios — sector books maintained side by side and switched in one click
  • History with overlay and stacked views from 1D to 1Y, filtered to gaining, losing, selected, or top movers since open
  • Data modes for price, position, % change, P/E and PEG, aggregated by total, average, or weighted average
  • Auto-refresh with pre/post-market coverage and a generated briefing

The interesting part

  • "Price physics" — treating price as motion: velocity as change per bar, acceleration as change in velocity, with adjustable smoothing
  • It surfaces inflections a price line flattens out — a decelerating rally and an accelerating one look identical on a chart and very different here
  • Composite opportunity scores combining velocity, acceleration, volume, percentage move, P/E and PEG into a single accumulate / neutral / reduce signal per holding

Pertussis epidemic model — 8-compartment SEIRS

Designed & built 120 hrs est → 24 hrs actual

Extension of an open epidemic-modeling framework · Jul 2026

Extended an existing epidemic-modeling framework to support pertussis, upgrading it to an 8-compartment SEIRS partial-immunity model with age-stratified dynamics — the structure the disease actually requires, since pertussis immunity wanes and age cohorts behave very differently.

Model

  • Age-stratified vaccination planner with per-age immunity and a CDC DTaP/Tdap schedule preset
  • Persistent campaign and coverage-by-age profiles, seeded from real county surveillance data
  • Observed-data comparison, seeding, and coarse calibration so the model is checkable against actual surveillance
  • A contacts-by-setting view for intervention reasoning

Validation & publication

  • A six-item scientific-validation protocol — inter-epidemic period, waning immunity, age distribution, vaccination response, ABC calibration fit, and face-validity bounds
  • Hardened for operational use: single-threaded native math and a resilient auto-restart launcher
  • Authored a journal-paper proposal for federal sponsors on extending an epidemic-modeling dashboard to a new pathogen with an AI coding assistant, with a systematic 2024–2026 literature review establishing the whitespace
Also delivered — an advanced SEIR measles simulation with disease parameters, multiple population segments, and time-phased interventions; and a Model Quality Assessment Tool evaluating infectious-disease models against a multi-criteria decision framework.

Spend-Plan Agent Validation Harness

Designed & built Applied AI rigor

Deterministic, non-LLM validation of an AI agent's compute path

If an AI agent produces a federal grant spend plan, someone has to be able to prove it is right. This harness grades agent output against an independently computed oracle — MTDC base, SF-424A object classes, projections — with explicit tolerances, and adds two layers on top.

Layers

  • A consistency layer that treats run-to-run drift as a defect
  • A metamorphic layer that validates behavior with no ground truth required

Why it matters

  • It provably discriminates: it catches a wrong-indirect-base bug the metamorphic layer misses, and a line-item-collapse bug the oracle misses
  • Neither layer alone is sufficient — which is the point, and the reason this is the kind of rigor that makes AI-generated analysis trustworthy in a grant-compliance context
